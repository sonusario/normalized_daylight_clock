Main hands
  • Real time: the main hands, filled. The digital clock shows this time.
  • Daylight time: dashed outline. Hide them and an inner ring of their hours appears instead.
  • White triangle: the two clocks agree there.
  • Black triangle: the clocks are furthest apart there.
  • Progress arc: from the main hour hand to the next triangle.
  • Since-last arc: thinner and dashed, from the last triangle up to the main hour hand. It never reaches past the first triangle behind the hand (on the 12-hour dial that can be one a full turn ahead).
  • Solid line: sunrise or sunset for the main hands. Dashed: for the other hands.
  • Light and dark fill: sun up and sun down, by the main hands.
  • Hour hands read the hour face. Minute and second hands read the 0 to 60 ring, millisecond hands the 0 to 1000 ring.
  • Ruler hands: one each for hours (h), minutes (m), and, when those hands are on, seconds (s) and milliseconds. Drag a knob, or drag in the ring-shaped band around the edge of the dial (the middle of the face and the corners outside the rings are left alone so the page scrolls); whichever ruler you drag snaps to the marks of the ring the pointer is closest to (the hour face, the 0 to 60 ring or the 0 to 1000 ring), so slide in or out for coarser or finer steps. A ruler between marks reads like a real clock hand. Together they set a time on the main clock, from 6 hours ago to 18 hours ahead (the next 12 on the 12-hour dial); the rate graph's strip says when that is on both clocks. Switching the main hands or the dial keeps the rulers on the same moment.
  • Shading and labels show the next full turn of the main hour hand (12 hours on the 12-hour dial), so the hand drags them along like a curtain: once it passes a label, that label flips to the next time it will reach that spot (PM to AM and back).
  • Dial style (Display tab): Unit circle draws the x and y axes and the unit circle, with angles counterclockwise from 3 o'clock in radians and degrees and their (cos, sin) points: inner puts it all on the dial's face, outer around the outside of the dial. Radar draws range rings in hours from the main hour hand, a sweep that turns with the fastest main hand on show (milliseconds, seconds or minutes), and a ping for each triangle on the dial: it sits nearer the middle the closer the hour hand is to that triangle (coming up or just passed), lights up as the sweep passes, and fades out by the time the sweep is halfway round.
  • Background (Display tab): Realistic sky follows the sun's colors through the day; each theme's gradient goes through its own night, dawn, day and dusk colors on the same schedule.

The tabs

  • Details: the other clock's time and today's date, day and night length, the real times of sunrise and sunset (always 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM on the daylight clock), and how long one daylight hour lasts right now. Sunrise is always 6:00 AM and sunset 6:00 PM; the hours between stretch or shrink to fit. With smooth daylight rate on, the clock speeds up and slows down gradually instead of snapping at sunrise and sunset, and still hits 6:00 at both. Real times are in the selected location's time zone. Tap the mini globe or the place name to change location.
  • Rate: how fast the daylight clock runs compared with real time, over 24 hours from 6 hours ago. Each step is the average speed over 5 real minutes. Hover or tap for a step's details; otherwise the strip under the graph shows the step at the ruler hands (or now, with the rulers off), on both clocks. The faint line is the other rate mode, and the triangles mark where the clocks agree or are furthest apart.
  • Display: which clock the main (filled) hands show, the smooth rate, the 12- or 24-hour dial, labels, which hands, lines and arcs are drawn, the ruler hands, label density, theme, background and dial style. Reset puts them all back.
  • Legend: this list of what each mark on the dial means.

About Daylight Clock

A clock that stretches the day to fit the sun: sunrise is always 6:00 AM and sunset is always 6:00 PM, wherever you are.
